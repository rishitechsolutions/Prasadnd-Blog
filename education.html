Leadership and motivation have never had a single, fixed meaning. They keep evolving, shaped by culture, experience, and the lens through which each person views them. Over the years, I have noticed that every leader, every scholar, and every organization interprets leadership differently. If these interpretations are subjective, then so are the ideas of what makes a leader "good" or "bad."

This led me to ask some fundamental questions of my own : What truly defines a leader? Who decides what effective leadership looks like? And on what grounds do we judge it?

These questions have followed me throughout my career and now guide my research. Instead of accepting ready-made definitions, I have chosen to explore leadership through my own study, evidence, and experience
